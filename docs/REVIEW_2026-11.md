# Review, November 2026

The owner asked, after the asset pass: where is the game breaking, where is it slow (because of assets or anything else), is every feature working, and was everything in the briefs covered. This is the answer. It was made on 2026-11-21 in the cloud container, which has no graphics card: everything about speed on a real GPU is taken from the Mac's own measurements (docs/PERFORMANCE.md) or marked as needing one.

## In short

- **One real crash was found, and fixed.** Skipping ahead through history killed the simulation the first time a large eruption struck a young town, and the world started again from nothing. It happened on the first long skip that was tried in the page, at 126 million years on the usual seed. It is fixed and tested (part 2). Apart from that, five whole worlds run from nothing through eight thousand years of history with every system on showed no error, no bad number and no broken link in the record, and every save loads back the same.
- **The assets are not what makes it slow.** The made trees have two to ten times the triangles of the generated ones, but the Mac measured that half a million extra triangles cost a third of a millisecond, and each kind of tree is held to 600,000 triangles on screen. What is fetched is small: 1.2 MB of plant models at the start, 2 MB of props the first time the camera comes close, 4.3 MB of sound when the music starts.
- **What is slow, or heavy, is elsewhere:** the picture on Ultra and High on the Mac (the Mac's study), the simulation on a slow phone at the fastest speed, and the memory taken by the kept copies of the world (part 3).
- **Against the briefs: nothing is missing without a decision.** Four items were dropped or replaced by the owner (religion). But about two thirds of the expansion and a fifth of the original 150 are simpler than the briefs describe, and twelve expansion rows say "done" for something that is recorded but not drawn. The full list is part 4.
- **Fixed during the review:** a line of game text that still said "their gods"; postcard mode never switched on its depth of field; the help said six speeds where there are seven; the battlefield view now uses the real campaign records; the museum now hangs the country's real paintings and tells the old stories of its bones; a sealed vault now holds the country's own written history. A test now reads the whole source for the words the owner ruled out.

## 2. Where it breaks

### The crash: skipping through history killed the world (fixed)

- **What happened.** A long skip (the "skip to" list, or any fast-forward that carries on after history has begun) stopped at about 126 million years on `amber-reef-101`: the browser console said `worker error Uncaught TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'title')`, the simulation worker closed and was started again, and the world was back at year 0 (or at the last autosave).
- **Why.** In a fast-forwarded history, and for the lesser countries of a crowded world, an ordinary event is not kept: `emit` hands back an event with no id (-1). A large eruption is such an event. `Civilization.witness` gave that id to a young town as "the story it tells of itself", and `civEvent('story')` then read the title of an event that does not exist.
- **Who it reached.** Anyone who skipped ahead past the start of history. It may also have happened, rarely, in a watched crowded world. It explains item 50 of `docs/OPEN_FINDINGS.md` ("a single fast-forward of 200 My ended with no towns at all").
- **The fix.** A town takes its story only from an event that is on record (`sim/civilization.ts` `witness`; a guard in `sim/simulation.ts` `civEvent`). A test in `tests/memory.test.ts` strikes a town with an unrecorded disaster.
- **Checked.** `scripts/checks/skipthrough.ts` steps a world in 15,000-year steps through the start of history and 40 million years on, as a skip does: five seeds on the small planet and `amber-reef-101` at full size (1,115 towns at 163 My) all run clean. Before the fix `amber-reef-101` died at 125.7 My.

### The page sweep (every panel, every tool)

`scripts/checks/reviewsweep.js` (barren world, then a world of animals) and `scripts/checks/reviewsweep-history.js` (a world in history) open all 24 panels, click every tab in each, and use all 17 tools, printing every console error, failed request and page error as it happens.

- **Barren world: all 24 panels and all 17 tools, no error of any kind.**
- **World of animals and world in history: not finished.** The first attempt was spoilt by the crash above (the world reset under it); the rerun on the fixed build was stopped when the owner paused the session. This is the first thing to run on resuming.
- One thing to watch for when it is rerun: in the spoilt attempt the page stopped answering somewhere in the panels after the world had reset. It may only have been the software renderer under load (three heavy jobs were running), so the scripts now name the panel if it happens again.
- Once, under the same load, the headless browser closed by itself in the middle of a props check. Three reruns were clean. Not counted as a fault of the game.

Known already and still open: the Mac session keeps the list in `docs/OPEN_FINDINGS.md` (written the same day at the owner's request). It is not repeated here. Nothing in this review contradicts it; the crash above is not on it because the Mac had not skipped through history since the change that exposed it.

On a real GPU nobody has yet seen: the asset pass (trees, props) or heard its sounds, the echo, heat and electric senses (the Mac's list says three of the four cannot be reached in a normal world), and the second sculpting pass of the animals.

## 3. Where it is slow

### The assets

| | Files fetched | Size | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant models | 9 | 1.21 MB | at the start |
| Props (tents, crops, boats, rocks) | 17 | 1.95 MB | the first time the camera comes within reach of the ground |
| Sound loops and thunder | 12 | 4.29 MB | when the music starts (the first click) |
| The game's own code | 3 | 2.4 MB (0.72 MB compressed) | at the start |

The whole `public/assets` folder is 37 MB and 771 files, of which the game names 59. The rest is never fetched; it only makes the repository and the published site larger. It can be pruned if the owner does not want the unused files kept at hand.

Triangles. The chosen trees have 1,200 to 2,900 triangles each, against 280 to 1,400 for the generated ones. In a forest seen from the ground the software renderer counted 2.96 million triangles submitted a frame with the models (356 pines standing). Each kind of tree is limited to 600,000 triangles on screen, which is what a full stand of the generated broadleaf already cost. The Mac's study measured what triangles cost on its GPU: hiding 553,000 triangles of animals saved 0.3 ms of a 21.8 ms frame. So the models should cost well under a millisecond there. **This needs one real measurement:** the forest ground view with and without `&noassets` on the Mac.

On a phone the plant budget is small (the Low preset draws few full models), so the models add little; not measured on a real phone.

### The simulation

Full-size planet, one core of this container, while two other jobs were running (so these are on the slow side):

| Stage | Cost | What the game asks |
|---|---|---|
| Before life, microbes, algae | 5 to 10 ms a step | |
| Animals, before history | 24 to 30 ms a step (15,000 years a step) | Epoch speed is 20 steps a second: 0.5 to 0.6 s of work each second |
| History | 2.9 ms a year | the History speed is 25 years a second: 0.07 s each second |

- On a laptop the fastest speed keeps up, with about half of one core to spare. **On a phone three or four times slower it cannot**: the world will run at perhaps half the speed shown. The worker also lets the years it owes pile up without limit while it is behind (`owed` in `tick`, `sim/worker.ts`), so after a slow stretch it will run flat out for a while. A cap of a few steps would fix that. It is in code the build session changed this week ("one seed is one world"), so it is handed to them in HANDOFF.md, not changed here.
- History itself is cheap. A crowded late world on the small test planet costs 2 to 9 ms for five years.

### Memory and saves

| Full-size world | A save weighs | To make | To load |
|---|---|---|---|
| New | 13.4 MB | 36 ms | |
| At the dawn of history (123 My) | 26.9 MB | 99 ms | 71 ms |
| 3,000 years later | 27.1 MB | 114 ms | 120 ms |

What it is made of at the dawn of history: the per-cell history log 8.9 MB, the species tables 6.4 MB (4 MB of it there from the first moment, before any species exists: the tables are saved at their full reserved size), the plant species 2.4 MB, the event log 1.8 MB, and the planet itself about 3 MB.

- The page keeps 12 copies of the world to go back to (5 on a phone), an era copy for each age, and the autosave. At 27 MB each that is 300 to 400 MB on a desktop late in a world. The build session has already made the long lists in these copies small; the typed arrays are the bulk now.
- **Cheap saving to hand on:** save the species tables only up to the rows in use (4 MB less in every copy from the first moment), and keep the per-cell history log out of the rewind copies or share it between them (it only grows). Not done here: `snapshot.ts` and the worker's copies are the build session's.
- Ten named save slots of 27 MB are 270 MB in the browser's storage. Browsers allow it on a desktop; a phone may refuse. Not tried on a phone.

### The picture

Nothing new was measured here (no GPU). The Mac's figures stand: on its M3 Pro, High runs at 46 to 51 frames a second, Medium at 61 to 74, Ultra at 17 to 20; the brief asked for 60 on a mid-range laptop. No real phone has been measured. docs/PERFORMANCE.md has what each part of the frame costs.
