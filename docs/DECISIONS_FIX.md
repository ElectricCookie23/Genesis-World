# Decisions of the fix session

Numbered F1, F2, ... so they never collide with `DECISIONS.md`, which the build session keeps. Earlier decisions of this session are in `DECISIONS.md` (the goals mode at 107, the star system and what follows at 121 to 128).

F1. **The linked world runs inside the first one's worker, in lockstep** (E98). Two workers exchanging messages would drift with the wall clock, and the pair would not repeat from its two seeds. So `sim.neighbor` is a whole simulation on a small grid, stepped with the same years, saved beside the first, and the exchange is a pure function of the two states. Linking costs one small world's worth of time, which is why it is something the player turns on. A world linked late is brought to the same age in long steps before the two are joined.

F2. **The 150 were audited by reading and by running, not by the checklist** (`docs/FEATURE_AUDIT.md`). An independent pass traced each feature from simulation to screen and ran six histories to see which events really fire: 123 work, 25 partly, 2 unclear, none missing. Most of the 25 are "coded but never happens": the look exists, the world never produces what triggers it. `scripts/checks/traitsweep.ts` measures those triggers over whole histories and is the test for any change to them.

F3. **Hunters are started, not waited for.** Three things were wrong at once: a species preyed on its own packs (a released hunter lost four times more to its own kind than to hunger), hunters ate a region bare and starved, and the path from grazer to hunter ran downhill all the way (a little more meat and a little less grass always paid worse). The first two are fixed in the rules. For the third, tuning the pay-off was tried and gave different answers every run, so a world of animals with no hunters now turns a few populations of an abundant part-meat-eater into hunters in one step (diet, boldness, eyes, size, legs together), and the ordinary rules decide whether they become a species and last. Measured: they do, in all three seeds.

F4. **A hunter is where meat pays double, not where the old line was.** The simulation, the cards and the chase all asked for a diet gene above 0.6. Meat and plant efficiency cross near 0.3, and at 0.45 meat gives twice what plants do: that is `HUNTER_DIET`, one constant for all three.

F5. **Senses and signals pay their way gradually.** Echolocation and electric sense did nothing below a threshold and cost nothing, so they drifted and never crossed it; glow and ornaments only cost; the larval-stage gene did nothing at all. Each now has a use that grows with the gene (finding food, being harder to surprise, finding a mate in the dark, display in company, young that do not eat their parents' food). The drawn thresholds are unchanged. The designer has sliders for all of them, so a player can also simply ask.

F6. **The sea moves enough to matter.** Ice takes 0.08 km of sea per unit of ice (was 0.035) and warm water stands higher (0.011 km per degree). The events for falling and rising seas, land bridges and invasions were all waiting behind a swing that never came. Two of the build session's migration tests were tied to one world and changed with it (the flood test now floods twice; both read the player's act from `godEvents` instead of "the last event").

F7. **A mind is kept out of the hunters' way.** With hunters in the world, the first peoples came much later in some seeds (one went from 90 to 308 My). Two causes: the rule that starts a hunting lineage was choosing the cleverest abundant animal and turning it into a hunter, and hunters were thinning the very herds a mind grows in. That rule now passes over animals with intelligence above 0.4, and wits in company count toward defence. Measured on five seeds at grid 21: history begins at 121 to 139 My in four and not by 400 My in one (before any of this: 91 to 124 My in four and 300 My in one). Which seed is the late one changes whenever the rules of life do, so tests that need a history no longer name a seed for it.

F8. **A country that has just ended does nothing more that year** (`civilization.ts`, the yearly loop). A succession can hand a country to another by an old marriage; the loop then went on to grow that country's towns and found daughters in its name, leaving living towns that belonged to a fallen nation. The build session's own test for that invariant caught it once the new rules made unions and migrations more frequent.

F9. **The scene is never multisampled, and the aurora is not drawn where it cannot be seen** (from `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, measured on the owner's GPU). With temporal anti-aliasing off the scene pass fell back to multisampling, which made "off" 1 to 14 ms a frame slower than "on"; it is now `samples: 0` always, with FXAA at the end when the temporal pass is off (Medium, and the last step of the step-down ladder). The aurora cost 2.3 ms from orbit over the day side; its mesh is hidden there and when there is no air.

F10. **Exposure is held by the bright end as well as the average.** Aiming at a mid-grey mean, a mostly dark planet with a wide belt of sunlit cloud asked for nearly double exposure and the belt went flat white. The cap from orbit used to hide this; raising that cap for the night side (decision 72) exposed it, twice, on the GPU. The 98th percentile of the metering cells is now held just under white. Day side from orbit: exposure 1.27 instead of 1.81; night side unchanged at the cap.
