# Decisions of the fix session

Numbered F1, F2, ... so they never collide with `DECISIONS.md`, which the build session keeps. Earlier decisions of this session are in `DECISIONS.md` (the goals mode at 107, the star system and what follows at 121 to 128).

F1. **The linked world runs inside the first one's worker, in lockstep** (E98). Two workers exchanging messages would drift with the wall clock, and the pair would not repeat from its two seeds. So `sim.neighbor` is a whole simulation on a small grid, stepped with the same years, saved beside the first, and the exchange is a pure function of the two states. Linking costs one small world's worth of time, which is why it is something the player turns on. A world linked late is brought to the same age in long steps before the two are joined.

F2. **The 150 were audited by reading and by running, not by the checklist** (`docs/FEATURE_AUDIT.md`). An independent pass traced each feature from simulation to screen and ran six histories to see which events really fire: 123 work, 25 partly, 2 unclear, none missing. Most of the 25 are "coded but never happens": the look exists, the world never produces what triggers it. `scripts/checks/traitsweep.ts` measures those triggers over whole histories and is the test for any change to them.

F3. **Hunters are started, not waited for.** Three things were wrong at once: a species preyed on its own packs (a released hunter lost four times more to its own kind than to hunger), hunters ate a region bare and starved, and the path from grazer to hunter ran downhill all the way (a little more meat and a little less grass always paid worse). The first two are fixed in the rules. For the third, tuning the pay-off was tried and gave different answers every run, so a world of animals with no hunters now turns a few populations of an abundant part-meat-eater into hunters in one step (diet, boldness, eyes, size, legs together), and the ordinary rules decide whether they become a species and last. Measured: they do, in all three seeds.

F4. **A hunter is where meat pays double, not where the old line was.** The simulation, the cards and the chase all asked for a diet gene above 0.6. Meat and plant efficiency cross near 0.3, and at 0.45 meat gives twice what plants do: that is `HUNTER_DIET`, one constant for all three.

F5. **Senses and signals pay their way gradually.** Echolocation and electric sense did nothing below a threshold and cost nothing, so they drifted and never crossed it; glow and ornaments only cost; the larval-stage gene did nothing at all. Each now has a use that grows with the gene (finding food, being harder to surprise, finding a mate in the dark, display in company, young that do not eat their parents' food). The drawn thresholds are unchanged. The designer has sliders for all of them, so a player can also simply ask.

F6. **The sea moves enough to matter.** Ice takes 0.08 km of sea per unit of ice (was 0.035) and warm water stands higher (0.011 km per degree). The events for falling and rising seas, land bridges and invasions were all waiting behind a swing that never came. Two of the build session's migration tests were tied to one world and changed with it (the flood test now floods twice; both read the player's act from `godEvents` instead of "the last event").

F7. **A mind is kept out of the hunters' way.** With hunters in the world, the first peoples came much later in some seeds (one went from 90 to 308 My). Two causes: the rule that starts a hunting lineage was choosing the cleverest abundant animal and turning it into a hunter, and hunters were thinning the very herds a mind grows in. That rule now passes over animals with intelligence above 0.4, and wits in company count toward defence. Measured on five seeds at grid 21: history begins at 121 to 139 My in four and not by 400 My in one (before any of this: 91 to 124 My in four and 300 My in one). Which seed is the late one changes whenever the rules of life do, so tests that need a history no longer name a seed for it.

F8. **A country that has just ended does nothing more that year** (`civilization.ts`, the yearly loop). A succession can hand a country to another by an old marriage; the loop then went on to grow that country's towns and found daughters in its name, leaving living towns that belonged to a fallen nation. The build session's own test for that invariant caught it once the new rules made unions and migrations more frequent.

F9. **The scene is never multisampled, and the aurora is not drawn where it cannot be seen** (from `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, measured on the owner's GPU). With temporal anti-aliasing off the scene pass fell back to multisampling, which made "off" 1 to 14 ms a frame slower than "on"; it is now `samples: 0` always, with FXAA at the end when the temporal pass is off (Medium, and the last step of the step-down ladder). The aurora cost 2.3 ms from orbit over the day side; its mesh is hidden there and when there is no air.

F10. **Exposure is held by the bright end as well as the average.** Aiming at a mid-grey mean, a mostly dark planet with a wide belt of sunlit cloud asked for nearly double exposure and the belt went flat white. The cap from orbit used to hide this; raising that cap for the night side (decision 72) exposed it, twice, on the GPU. The 98th percentile of the metering cells is now held just under white. Day side from orbit: exposure 1.27 instead of 1.81; night side unchanged at the cap.

F11. **Animals get anatomy in the profile, not more parts** (first step of `docs/CREATURE_SCULPTING.md`; owner: "why is everything so low poly?"). The body is still one skin lofted along the spine, but its depth and width now follow landmarks (deep chest, waist, hips; a hunter leaner than a grazer; a swimmer spindle-shaped), sections are rounded boxes where the body is a barrel, the back has a spine line, and the flank swells into a shoulder or haunch where each leg joins. Legs have a heavy thigh, a knee, a lean shin and an ankle. The head is a braincase, a brow and a muzzle that drops and narrows, with the eyes set into it. Twice the points round and along (about 3,000 triangles for a plain animal, was 1,400; the performance study measured all animals in view at under 0.4 ms). `scripts/checks/speciessheet.ts` draws any set of genomes from the side and at three-quarters without the game, and `docs/asset-previews/species-sheet-before.jpg` and `-after.jpg` are the same eight animals. Not done yet from the proposal: blended joints, feet by lifestyle, ears and jaws, a part library, contact shadows.

F12. **The older look list, worked through** (owner: "do the hand off notes thing from much before ... then sculpting last"). Each item was a line in a GPU review; all are software-checked only.
- *Stars by day:* the veil that hides stars from inside the air held only to 1.02 radii, which a camera over high ground passes; it now holds to 1.035. Satellites had no veil at all and were the "one or two stars" over a town at noon.
- *Trees:* trunks and boughs have more sides and segments, the trunk spreads into its roots, every limb is closed with a short rounded end (open tube ends were the "square-cut stubs"), each leaf card has its own tone and a darker midrib, turned shapes (stalks, caps) are 1.8 times rounder, bulbs are round balls, and coral branches curve, swell and sometimes fork in three.
- *Waders:* water now clouds what stands in it (an extra `exp(-1800 * path)`), so legs fade below the surface instead of showing to the toes.
- *Snow:* deep snow is no longer broken by the detail noise or shed by moderate slopes (the gravel look at 5 km).
- *Sea:* each set of waves fades out before its tile is small enough on screen to repeat as a grid (the diamond pattern from mid height).
- *Borders:* the Night Map's border line is read with a slow sideways wander of about a third of a cell, so it no longer steps along the hexagons.
- *Rivers:* `groundRadiusNode` smooths the detail and cuts the ground slightly along rivers (`setGroundRivers`), for the land and for everything placed on it, so a river lies in a bed instead of over the bumps.
- *Not found:* the white smudge over tree lines. It is probably a low cloud touching high ground rather than the mist term, but it was not reproduced here.

F13. **Rules that nothing ever met are loosened to what worlds actually produce** (audit items 62, 109, 110, 128, 135). Each was a threshold set by guess and never checked against a run: oxygen 30 percent for the age of giants (worlds level off at 24 to 25), three strict gene conditions together for cave dwellers, a volcano below 0.5 km for a vent (most worlds had none), a glowing plant thirstier than any that grew for a mushroom, a brood size and glow no sea animal reached for a mass hatching. Each is now set from measured histories (`traitsweep.ts`), and two were given a mechanism where there was only a label: rich air favours large bodies, and every world has vents on its deep sea floor. Left for the build session, whose file it now is: ruins drawn where a town stood (50) and temples turned toward the viewer (46), both in `buildings.ts`. Left as they are: 12 and 144 (the simulation has populations, not individual animals), 40 (same seed, different history in the browser), 129, 133.

F14. **Section 9 is one module of ten small mechanisms, each with a number the world can act on** (`sim/strange.ts`). None of them is a second simulation. Strata are a list written from the event log. A pole flip is a date, a duration and three consequences. Microbes are one pressure per region. The hive mind is one species and a slowly filling measure. The swarm is a period and four stages of a people's understanding. Scars are one value per region that halves in 400 years. The pulse is three terms. Where the brief asked for a picture that would need new rendering (light pulses across a continent, animals in a trance, scars painted on the land) the system is built and recorded and the picture is left, marked simplified in PROGRESS.md.
F15. **Short-cycle things are only told when time runs slowly.** The swarm returns every few thousand years and scars heal over centuries, but most of a world's life passes in steps of fifteen thousand years. In such a step the swarm is not told at all and scars heal completely: they belong to history, when the simulation runs year by year.

F16. **The far future runs on one clock and the present civilization** (Section 8, `sim/future.ts`). Sections 5 to 7 are not built yet, so Section 8 could not wait for an industrial and near-future model to stand on. It stands on what exists: the space-age milestone, the living towns and their knowledge. One clock counts the years a people lives on with those means (it stops when there is no one, and a last war resets it). Each system waits for its year, drawn from the seed, and some happen only in some worlds (uplift, machine minds, upload, the star swarm), so futures differ. When the build session reaches Section 7, the thresholds here (`maxTech` 1.0 to 1.12) should be moved onto whatever it uses for the ages after the space age.
F17. **Made, revived and raised species are ordinary species with a mark.** They are released through the same routine as a designed animal (`sim.releaseSpecies`) and then live or die by the usual rules; the mark (`future.origins`) is only shown. An uplifted species is added to the list of thinking species and the existing settlement rules do the rest. A revived species is set free where its kind last lived.
F18. **An ending is a record, with one consequence.** It does not stop the world: it names how that history came out, changes the towns or the land once, and the simulation goes on (ruins, wildlife, the red giant are all still to come).
F19. **The far future does not advance during the long peace.** The build session's model ends a fast-forwarded history in a peace in which no nations or people are added and the chronicle is all but silent, and its test holds that. An uplifted species founding a town during the peace broke it. So Section 8's clock runs only while history is live (watched at the pace of history); a world jumped through ages keeps whatever its people had built when the peace settled.

F20. **Fuel is laid down by the world's own history, from its first forests** (Section 7, `sim/industry.ts`). Every step, each region with old wet forest gains coal and each shallow living sea gains oil, with the first and last year and (for coal) the plant that grew there. Nothing is invented when industry begins: the richest regions are opened and each says what it was. A world that never grew forests has no coal.
F21. **Section 7 adds to the civilization it finds; it does not model an economy.** The existing rules already have an industrial age, emissions and a space age. This section dates and records what follows from them (electric light, the first broadcast, the network, debris, a moon base, a council, a sky watch) and gives four of them consequences that can be measured: debris closes the sky, a council cuts emissions, a sky watch turns rocks aside, and warming is counted separately from the world's own. Factories, railways, smog and cables are not drawn; those rows are marked simplified.
